# legible (slim)

Write so a reader understands each sentence on the first read. Two things make readers stop and reread: a word they didn't predict, and a subject held too long before its verb.

Before you finish, scan the sentences that carry your main point and fix only the ones a first-time reader would stall on:

1. Name the real actor or effect. Write "you have to remember it", not "chaos expects you to remember it"; write "causes a problem", not "bites".
2. Reach the verb within about seven words of its subject. Move long lists and modifiers after the verb, or split the sentence.
3. Keep "that" where its absence could misread the sentence ("she knew that the answer was wrong"). Drop it when the clause is short and obvious.
4. Unstack three or more nouns with a verb or "of/for": "phones must be off during class", not "class-time shutdown requirement".
5. Use the verb phrase, not a noun turned verb: "put it in a footnote", not "footnote it".
6. Use the common phrase, not a coined one. If a new term is needed, define it once.
7. Say the literal thing, not a metaphor that must be decoded.

Leave conventional terms, idiom and the author's voice alone. Most sentences will pass. Never add facts, and never pad.
